## 0. Headline: what is actually in force on 2 November 2026 (read this first)

### Takeaway
The DPDP Act's substantive fiduciary duties (notice, consent, security, breach, retention, rights, children, cross-border) and Rules 3, 5-16, 22, 23 are NOT yet in force; they commence 18 months after Gazette publication, i.e. on or about 13 May 2027 (sources differ 12/13/14 May). Until then the IT Act s.43A and SPDI Rules 2011 (health data = SPDI) and the CERT-In 2022 Directions are the live regime. Rule 4 (Consent Manager registration) commences on or about 13 Nov 2026 and does not oblige a small fiduciary to do anything.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 1: Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 commence on Gazette publication; Rule 4 one year after; "Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 shall come into force eighteen months after the date of publication of this Gazette." Rules published 13 Nov 2025 — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf) (Rule 1 summarised by [dpdpa.com Rule 1](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule1.html))
- Act commencement notification (13 Nov 2025), per a law-firm summary: immediate = s.2, ss.18-26, s.38, s.44(1),(3) (plus Rules 2, 17-21); after one year = s.6(9) (Consent Manager registration) and s.27(1)(d) and Rule 4; after 18 months = ss.3, 4, 5, 6(1)-(8),(10), 7, 8-10, 11-15, 16, 17, 27 (except (1)(d)), and s.44(2) — [Acuity Law](https://acuitylaw.co.in/?p=6681). Same split reported by [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/). Note Acuity computes dates as 12 Nov 2026 / 12 May 2027; other sources 13 May 2027 and one 14 May 2027 — the exact calendar day is computed, not stated in the instrument ([dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/blog/dpdp-18-months-to-12-months-proposal)).
- The Data Protection Board provisions (ss.18-26) and the Rules on its constitution (17-21) are in force since 13 Nov 2025 — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/). Board appointments: MeitY circular of 6 May 2026 invited applications for a Chairperson and four Members; I found no reliable report that all appointments are complete or that the complaint portal is live — [Medianews4u](https://www.medianews4u.com/government-establishes-data-protection-board-of-india-under-dpdp-act-invites-applications-for-key-roles/); [Khurana & Khurana](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/india-s-data-protection-board-the-enforcer-that-isn-t-there-yet) (low-confidence secondary).
- Proposal to compress the 18-month window to 12 months (reported Jan-Feb 2026: Business Standard 22 Jan 2026, S.S. Rana 13 Feb 2026, comments due 4 Feb 2026; scope unclear — SDFs only or all fiduciaries) — [S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meity-may-cut-compliance-timeline-for-key-dpdp-rules-to-12-months-126012201293_1.html). As of mid-Oct 2026 a tracker found no notified instrument changing commencement; a Lok Sabha written answer of 12 Aug 2026 still described an "eighteen month transition period" — [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/blog/dpdp-18-months-to-12-months-proposal) (secondary; it states it did not search the eGazette).
- Digital Personal Data Protection (Removal of Difficulties) Order, 2026 (S.O. 5458(E), made 5 Oct 2026, gazetted ~7 Oct 2026): two editorial fixes only — inserts "of" in s.9(1) ("personal data of a child or of a person with a disability") and replaces "audit" with "data audit" in s.10(2)(c)(ii); does not change commencement — [CyberPeace](https://cyberpeace.org/resources/blogs/small-words-big-consequences-reading-the-dpdp-removal-of-difficulties-order-2026); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/?p=139777); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/?p=398941). Dates differ slightly across sources (5 vs 6 vs 7 Oct).
- A Rules corrigendum G.S.R. 113(E) exists (Feb 2026); content not retrieved — [Simpliance listing](https://simpliance.in/India/EHS/govt_notification/Central/notification-regarding-corrigendum-to-the-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-8637)
- s.38(1)-(2): the Act is "in addition to and not in derogation of any other law", but on conflict the DPDP Act prevails — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Applicability: "Data Fiduciary" = any "person" who alone or with others determines purpose and means (s.2(i)); "person" includes an individual (s.2(s)); Act applies to digital personal data processed in India, and outside India if connected to offering goods/services to Data Principals in India (s.3(a)-(b)); excluded are processing for personal/domestic purposes and data made public by the Data Principal (s.3(c)). A commercial app run by an individual is a Data Fiduciary — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf); [dpdpa.com s.3 page](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-1/section3.html)
- Section 17(1)(d): chapters II (except s.8(1),(5)), III and s.16 do not apply where data of Data Principals NOT within India is processed under a contract with a person outside India by a person based in India (offshore-processing carve-out; aimed at B2B outsourcing, not a B2C app) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Section 17(3): Central Government may notify Data Fiduciaries "including startups" as exempt from s.5, s.8(3), s.8(7), ss.10 and 11 — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf). I found no confirmation that any s.17(3) notification has been issued; commentators describe MSMEs/startups as covered by default — [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-compliance-for-startups-msmes/)

### Inferences
- For a live app in Nov 2026: the DPDP duties are a build-toward-May-2027 target, not current legal obligations; the legally binding items today are SPDI Rules (health data), CERT-In Directions, IT Act s.43A liability, COTPA. Good practice is to ship DPDP-ready notice/consent/retention now so no re-consent campaign is needed (s.5(2) requires a legacy-user notice "as soon as reasonably practicable" after commencement for consents already given).
- Wording any user-facing page as "we comply with DPDP" before 13 May 2027 is a claim about readiness, not current legal compliance; "DPDP-aligned" is safer.
- Unclear whether the EU-resident users are within DPDP: a fiduciary based in India processes in India (s.3(a)), so the Act probably applies to all users regardless of nationality (interpretation); s.17(1)(d) does not help for B2C data.

### Gaps
- No official MeitY/eGazette check was possible for any post-Feb-2026 amendment to the Rules; absence of a found amendment is not proof of none.
- Exact calendar date of commencement (12/13/14 May 2027) is not stated in the instruments.
- Status/composition of the Data Protection Board and complaint portal as of Nov 2026 unverified.

## 1. DPDP notice: exact contents, language, plain-English and standalone requirements

### Takeaway
Act s.5 plus Rule 3 (in force ~13 May 2027) require a standalone, plain-language notice with at minimum an itemised description of the personal data, the specified purpose(s) and a specific description of goods/services or uses enabled, plus the link to the app/site and the means to withdraw consent, exercise rights and complain to the Board; it must be offered in English or any of the 22 Eighth Schedule languages.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 3 verbatim: notice must "(a) be presented and be understandable independently of any other information that has been, is or may be made available by such Data Fiduciary; (b) give, in clear and plain language, a fair account of the details necessary to enable the Data Principal to give specific and informed consent for the processing of her personal data, which shall include, at the minimum, -- (i) an itemised description of such personal data; and (ii) the specified purpose or purposes of, and specific description of the goods or services to be provided or uses to be enabled by, such processing; and (c) give, the particular communication link for accessing the website or app, or both, of such Data Fiduciary, and a description of other means, if any, using which such Data Principal may -- (i) withdraw her consent, with the ease of doing so being comparable to that with which such consent was given; (ii) exercise her rights under the Act; and (iii) make a complaint to the Board." — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Act s.5(1): every consent request must be "accompanied or preceded by a notice" informing (i) the personal data and purpose, (ii) how she can exercise rights under s.6(4) (withdrawal) and s.13 (grievance), (iii) how she can complain to the Board — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Act s.5(3): the Data Fiduciary "shall give the Data Principal the option to access the contents of the notice ... in English or any language specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution" (22 languages) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf). Same language option repeated in s.6(3) for every consent request.
- Act s.6(3): consent requests must be in "clear and plain language" and provide "the contact details of a Data Protection Officer, where applicable, or of any other person authorised by the Data Fiduciary to respond to any communication from the Data Principal for the purpose of exercise of her rights" — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Rule 9: prominently publish on website/app, and mention in every response to a rights communication, the business contact information of the DPO "if applicable, or a person who is able to answer on behalf of the Data Fiduciary the questions of the Data Principal about the processing of her personal data" — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Legacy users (consent given before commencement): s.5(2) requires a notice with the same three items "as soon as it is reasonably practicable"; processing may continue until withdrawal — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Rules do not prescribe a form, font, or translation vendor; Rule 3 is the only prescription on notice content — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Burden of proof: if consent is disputed, the fiduciary must prove notice was given and consent obtained per the Act (s.6(10)) — [dpdpa.com s.6 summary](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section6.html) (consistent with Act text; Act s.6(10) not separately re-read in PDF).

### Inferences
- Checklist for the Smoke Ledger notice (standalone, not buried in Terms/Privacy Policy): list each data item (cigarette log entries with timestamps, spend amounts, mood, trigger, craving, free-text notes, account email/identifier, device/analytics data if opted in, AI-chat payloads: aggregates and recent entries); state each purpose separately (core logging/tracking; opt-in analytics; opt-in AI chat via Anthropic) with a plain description of the feature it enables; link to app/website; state in-app path for withdrawal (e.g., Settings toggles), rights and the Board complaint route; show the contact person (Rule 9).
- A single notice that bundles analytics and AI chat consent into account-creation would likely fail "specific" consent; separate, itemised, per-purpose notices (just-in-time at the opt-in) fit Rule 3(a) "independently of any other information".
- Eighth Schedule languages: no rule says all 22 must be pre-translated; s.5(3) says the Principal must have the "option to access" the notice in those languages. Practical compliance would be a machine-translated or translated version for each of the 22; whether quality/authenticity standards apply is untested (interpretation). English-only is a likely non-compliance after commencement.

### Gaps
- No Board guidance or enforcement precedent yet on what counts as "itemised" or "specific description" or on whether a notice offered via a translation toggle satisfies s.5(3).
- Whether a notice must be re-presented when purposes change — not addressed in the Rules text.

## 2. Consent standard, withdrawal, Consent Managers

### Takeaway
Consent must be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action", limited to data necessary for the specified purpose; withdrawal must be as easy as giving; Consent Managers are optional intermediaries and impose nothing on a small fiduciary.

### Cited Findings
- s.6(1): "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action", signifying agreement "for the specified purpose and be limited to such personal data as is necessary for such specified purpose" (illustration: telemedicine app cannot bundle contact-list access) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- s.6(2): any part of consent that infringes the Act/Rules/other law is invalid to that extent (illustration: waiver of right to complain to the Board is void) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- s.6(4): right to withdraw at any time "with the ease of doing so being comparable to the ease with which such consent was given"; s.6(5) consequences borne by Principal, earlier processing stays lawful — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- s.6(6): on withdrawal the fiduciary must "within a reasonable time" cease processing and cause its Data Processors to cease, unless processing is required/authorised by law — [dpdpa.com s.6 summary](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section6.html)
- s.8(7): on withdrawal or when purpose no longer served, erase personal data (and cause processors to erase) unless retention is required by law — [dpdpa.com s.8 summary](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section8.html)
- Consent Managers: s.2(g) defines a person registered with the Board acting as single point of contact; s.6(7) a Data Principal "may give, manage, review or withdraw" consent through one (permissive); s.6(9) and Rule 4/First Schedule govern registration (commencing one year after publication, ~13 Nov 2026) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf); [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- No Rule obliges a fiduciary (small or large) to integrate with or accept a Consent Manager; Rule 14(1) and (3) mention Consent Managers only "where applicable" — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Lawful grounds: s.4 processing only per the Act and for a "lawful purpose" (not expressly forbidden by law) with consent or "certain legitimate uses" — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- Account creation can legitimately be conditioned on consent for the processing necessary to provide the logging service; analytics and AI chat must be separate, opt-in, unticked, with no loss of core service when declined ("unconditional", "limited to ... necessary" data).
- "As easy as giving" means in-app toggle withdrawal for analytics/AI chat, not an email-only route; Rule 3(c)(i) mirrors this.
- Withdrawing AI-chat consent should also trigger a stop on sending data to Anthropic and (per s.6(6)/8(7)) instruct that processor to erase any retained copies — flag for processor terms.
- Consent Managers: ignore; optional (interpretation from s.6(7) wording).

### Gaps
- Technical form of the "clear affirmative action" (pre-checked boxes, scroll-wrap) not prescribed; no Board guidance yet.
- Detailed s.6(6) timing ("reasonable time") undefined.

## 3. Legitimate uses; special status of health/smoking data; SPDI Rules status

### Takeaway
Self-logged smoking, craving and mood data is processed on consent (s.7(a) "voluntary provision" is arguably available but should not be relied on for optional features). DPDP has no sensitive-data category (confirmed from Act text; commentary agrees); but until ~13 May 2027 the SPDI Rules 2011 treat "physical, physiological and mental health condition" and "medical records and history" as SPDI, adding written-consent, privacy-policy and grievance-officer duties; s.43A and the SPDI Rules fall away when s.44(2) commences.

### Cited Findings
- s.7 lists nine "certain legitimate uses" without consent: (a) voluntary provision for a specified purpose where the Principal has not indicated non-consent to use; (b)-(c) State benefits/functions; (d) legal disclosure to State; (e) judgments/orders; (f) medical emergency (threat to life or immediate threat to health); (g) epidemic/public health; (h) disaster/public order; (i) employment — [dpdpa.com s.7 summary](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section7.html). Only (a) is plausibly relevant to a consumer app; (f)/(g) concern medical emergencies/outbreaks.
- No "sensitive personal data" or "special category" definition exists in the DPDP Act (the defined terms s.2 do not include one); commentary agrees: "the DPDP framework has No separate statutory category of sensitive personal data" — [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/what-happens-spdi-rules-after-dpdp-enforcement/); health apps still covered — [Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas blog](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2024/06/mind-your-meds-and-metrics-navigating-the-indian-health-data-protection-labyrinth/), [Samagra Law/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/healthcare/1722484/health-data-the-dpdp-actnavigating-indias-healthcare-privacy-frontier)
- Nature of data does matter for penalties and SDF designation: s.33(2)(b) Board considers "the type and nature of the personal data affected by the breach"; s.10(1)(a) SDF factors include "volume and sensitivity of personal data processed" — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- SPDI Rules Rule 3 verbatim list: "(i) password; (ii) financial information such as Bank account or credit card or debit card or other payment instrument details; (iii) physical, physiological and mental health condition; (iv) sexual orientation; (v) medical records and history; (vi) Biometric information; (vii) any detail relating to the above clauses as provided to body corporate for providing service; and (viii) any of the information received under above clauses by body corporate for processing..." — [SPDI Rules PDF](https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2011/IT_Rules_2011.pdf)
- SPDI Rule 4: publish a privacy policy covering type of data, purpose, disclosure (Rule 6) and security practices (Rule 8). Rule 5(1): obtain consent "in writing through letter or Fax or email" from the provider regarding purpose before collecting SPDI; 5(2) lawful purpose + necessity; 5(3) tell the person of the fact, purpose, recipients, name/address of collecting and retaining agencies; 5(4) no retention longer than required; 5(6) allow review/correction; 5(7) option not to provide and to withdraw consent (in writing); 5(9) designate a Grievance Officer, publish name and contact details, redress "within one month" — [SPDI Rules PDF](https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2011/IT_Rules_2011.pdf)
- SPDI Rule 6: disclosure of SPDI to a third party needs prior permission unless agreed in contract or legally required; Rule 7: cross-border transfer of SPDI only to entities ensuring the same level of protection and only if necessary to perform the contract or with consent; Rule 8: reasonable security = documented information-security programme; IS/ISO/IEC 27001 is one example of a standard — [SPDI Rules PDF](https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2011/IT_Rules_2011.pdf)
- "Body corporate" (s.43A explanation) means "any company and includes a firm, sole proprietorship or other association of individuals engaged in commercial or professional activities" — quoted in [CERT-In FAQ Q25](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/FAQs_on_CyberSecurityDirections_May2022.pdf). So an individual running a commercial app as a sole proprietor is within s.43A/SPDI.
- Repeal timing: DPDP Act s.44(2) omits IT Act s.43A and s.87(2)(ob) (the rule-making power under which the SPDI Rules were framed). s.44(2) commences with the 18-month tranche (~13 May 2027); until then s.43A and SPDI Rules remain in force and "both frameworks effectively coexist" — [Act PDF s.44(2) text](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf); [Acuity Law](https://acuitylaw.co.in/?p=6681); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/what-happens-spdi-rules-after-dpdp-enforcement/) (dated Sept 2026: "The DPDP Act has not repealed the SPDI Rules yet")

### Inferences
- Smoking logs combined with mood/craving/notes are best treated as SPDI category (iii) "physical, physiological and mental health condition" (interpretation; no case law on tobacco-use logs specifically). Cautious approach: apply SPDI rules now (privacy policy, email/written consent, named Grievance Officer with one-month limit, ISO 27001-style documented security programme, cross-border consent) — this is cheap and overlaps DPDP.
- Technically the SPDI rules were "framed under" a power that is omitted, not formally repealed; commentary (KS&K) says they lose their statutory foundation on commencement (interpretation). No saving clause was found; expect the SPDI duties to lapse May 2027 while DPDP's start.
- Free-text notes may contain third-party or other health information — practical reason to treat the whole dataset at high sensitivity.

### Gaps
- No Indian authority classifying tobacco-use logs as "health condition" under SPDI; interpretation only.
- Whether SPDI Rule 5(1) "writing" is satisfied by in-app click consent: debated, no authoritative ruling retrieved.

## 4. Data Principal rights, response timelines, grievance redressal, nominee

### Takeaway
The Act gives four rights (access summary s.11, correction/completion/updating/erasure s.12, grievance redressal s.13, nominate s.14). The Rules set only one hard number: a published grievance-response period "not exceeding ninety days" (Rule 14(3)); there is no statutory SLA for access/erasure requests; fiduciary must publish how to make requests and contact details.

### Cited Findings
- s.11: access to (a) a summary of personal data processed and processing activities, (b) identities of all other Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors with whom data has been shared plus description of data shared, (c) other prescribed information; applies to a fiduciary to whom she previously consented (including s.7(a)) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf). Therefore the notice and records should list processors, i.e. Supabase and Anthropic.
- s.12(1)-(3): correction, completion, updating; erasure on request "unless retention of the same is necessary for the specified purpose or for compliance with any law" — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- s.13: readily available grievance redressal; respond "within such period as may be prescribed"; Principal must exhaust grievance redressal before approaching the Board (s.13(3)) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- s.14: nominate another individual to exercise rights "in the event of death or incapacity" (incapacity = unsoundness of mind or infirmity of body) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Rule 14(1): prominently publish on website/app "(a) the details of the means using which a Data Principal may make a request for the exercise of such rights; and (b) the particulars, if any, such as the username or other identifier ... required to identify her"; 14(2) requests go to the fiduciary she previously consented to; 14(3) publish the grievance redressal system with response time "within a reasonable period not exceeding ninety days" and implement "appropriate technical and organisational measures" to meet it; 14(4) nominee exercise per terms of service and applicable law; 14(5) "identifier" includes email address, mobile number, etc. — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 9 contact person (see section 1); s.8(10): "effective mechanism" for grievances; s.8(9): publish DPO/contact details — [dpdpa.com s.8 summary](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section8.html)
- Rule 14 sets no separate deadline for access/correction/erasure requests (the draft's and some commentary's "30/60 day" figures do not appear in the final Rule text I read) — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf). Note: [dpdpa.com s.11 page](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-3/section11.html) commentary claims 7-day/30-day timelines and a "Rule 13" — this does not match the Gazette text; disregard.
- Data Principal duties (s.15): no false/frivolous complaints etc.; breach penalised up to Rs 10,000 — [Act PDF Schedule](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Existing Smoke Ledger export and account deletion map to s.11/s.12(3); SPDI Rule 5(9) one-month grievance limit applies until repeal — [SPDI Rules PDF](https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2011/IT_Rules_2011.pdf)

### Inferences
- Choose one published grievance SLA that satisfies both regimes: acknowledge quickly, resolve within 30 days (SPDI one month), absolute cap under 90 days (Rule 14(3)).
- Provide a nominee mechanism: at minimum a documented process (e.g., email with proof of death/incapacity and nominee identity) described in terms of service; Rule 14(4) requires only that the Principal can nominate "using the means and furnishing the particulars required by" the fiduciary.
- Each rights-response email must carry the Rule 9 contact line.

### Gaps
- No prescribed deadline or fee rules for access/erasure; an "appropriate" internal SLA is a judgement call.
- Identity verification standard for requests not prescribed.

## 5. Personal data breach notification (Board and principals)

### Takeaway
Rule 7 (in force ~13 May 2027): notify each affected Principal "without delay" with five specified items, and notify the Board "without delay" with a first description, then a detailed report within 72 hours (extendable by the Board on written request). Failure to notify attracts the second-highest penalty tier (up to Rs 200 crore). Separately, CERT-In's 6-hour reporting direction is already in force.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: "personal data breach" = unauthorised processing or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction or loss of access to personal data that compromises confidentiality, integrity or availability (s.2(u)) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- s.8(6): intimate Board and each affected Data Principal "in such form and manner as may be prescribed"; Schedule item 2: up to Rs 200 crore for failing — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Rule 7(1) to each affected Principal "in a concise, clear and plain manner and without delay, through her user account or any mode of communication registered by her": (a) description of breach incl. nature, extent, timing; (b) consequences likely to arise for her; (c) mitigation measures; (d) safety measures she may take; (e) business contact information of a person who can respond — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 7(2) to the Board: (a) "without delay", a description incl. nature, extent, timing, location and likely impact; (b) "within seventy-two hours of becoming aware of the breach, or within such longer period as the Board may allow on a request made in writing": (i) updated and detailed information, (ii) broad facts, events, circumstances and reasons, (iii) mitigation measures, (iv) findings regarding the person who caused the breach, (v) remedial measures to prevent recurrence, (vi) a report regarding intimations given to affected Data Principals — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- There is no materiality or risk threshold: every personal data breach is notifiable (unlike GDPR art.33 "unlikely to result in risk") — reading of Rule 7 text above; consistent with commentary [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/responding-to-data-breaches-key-obligations-under-the-dpdpa-2023-and-dpdp-rules-2025) (secondary)
- CERT-In Directions (in force since 27 June 2022): report Annexure I incidents (includes "Data Breach", "Data Leak", "Unauthorised access of IT systems/data", "Attacks on Application such as ... E-Commerce", "Attacks or malicious/suspicious activities affecting Cloud computing systems/servers/software/applications", "Attacks or malicious/suspicious activities ... related to Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning") to CERT-In "within 6 hours of noticing such incidents or being brought to notice", via incident@cert-in.org.in, 1800-11-4949 — [CERT-In Directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf). Partial reporting allowed: FAQ Q30 says provide information available and the rest later "within reasonable time" — [CERT-In FAQs](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/FAQs_on_CyberSecurityDirections_May2022.pdf)
- SPDI Rules contain no breach-notification duty but Rule 8(1) requires the body corporate to demonstrate its documented security controls "in the event of an information security breach" — [SPDI Rules PDF](https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2011/IT_Rules_2011.pdf)

### Inferences
- Draft one incident runbook with three clocks: CERT-In 6h (now), Board "without delay" + 72h (from May 2027), user notice "without delay" with the 5 Rule 7(1) elements. Supabase/Anthropic incidents count if personal data in the fiduciary's control is compromised (s.8(1) fiduciary remains responsible for processors).
- Some commentary cites "48 hours" for startups; that is wrong against the Rule text (72 hours) — ignore it.

### Gaps
- Board's prescribed channel/form for intimation not yet published (Board digital office Rule 20 in force; portal status unverified).
- Whether the 72-hour clock runs from awareness by the processor or the fiduciary is not specified (interpretation: fiduciary's awareness, but contract should require prompt processor alerts).

## 6. Retention, erasure, log retention, 48-hour pre-erasure notice

### Takeaway
Two different retention floors apply to every fiduciary: Rule 6(1)(e) (one year of logs and personal data for breach detection/investigation) and Rule 8(3) (one year minimum of personal data, associated traffic data and logs from the date of processing, for Seventh Schedule government-access purposes, then erasure). The inactivity-based erasure with 48-hour advance notice (Rule 8(1)-(2), Third Schedule) applies only to e-commerce (2 crore+ users), online gaming intermediaries (50 lakh+) and social media intermediaries (2 crore+) with 3-year inactivity; Smoke Ledger is out of scope.

### Cited Findings
- s.8(7): erase when consent is withdrawn or the specified purpose is no longer served, whichever is earlier, unless retention is required by law, and cause processors to erase — [dpdpa.com s.8 summary](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section8.html); s.8(8): purpose deemed no longer served after a prescribed period of non-engagement (class-specific). s.12(3): erasure on request unless necessary for the specified purpose or legally required — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Rule 8(1)-(2) and Third Schedule: classes = e-commerce entity with not less than two crore registered users in India; online gaming intermediary with not less than fifty lakh; social media intermediary with not less than two crore; period "Three years from the date on which the Data Principal last approached the Data Fiduciary ... or the commencement of the [Rules], whichever is latest"; at least 48 hours before erasure the fiduciary must inform the Principal that data will be erased unless she logs in or otherwise initiates contact — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 8(3) verbatim: "a Data Fiduciary shall retain, in respect of any processing of personal data undertaken by it or on its behalf by a Data Processor, such personal data, associated traffic data and other logs of the processing for a minimum period of one year from the date of such processing, for the purposes as specified in the Seventh Schedule, after which the Data Fiduciary shall cause such personal data and logs to be erased, unless further retention is required for compliance with any other law". Illustration Case 1: e-book platform must keep order details, personal data and logs for at least one year "even if X deletes her account"; Case 2: fiduciary must ensure its cloud provider processor also retains data and logs for one year — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Seventh Schedule purposes (for Rule 8(3) and Rule 23 information calls): (1) State use for sovereignty/security of the State; (2) State use for performing functions or disclosing information under any law; (3) assessment for notifying SDFs — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 6(1)(e): to enable detection of unauthorised access, investigation, remediation and continued processing, "retain such logs and personal data for a period of one year, unless compliance with any law ... requires otherwise" — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 23: Central Government may require any Data Fiduciary or intermediary to furnish information for Seventh Schedule purposes; may bar disclosure to the Principal where sovereignty/security are at stake — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Law-firm reading: Rule 8(3) is a "universal minimum" retention for all fiduciaries; any retention beyond must be justified under the Act — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/) (interpretation)
- CERT-In 180-day rolling retention of ICT-system logs (in Indian jurisdiction per the Directions; FAQ Q35 says logs may be stored outside India "as long as the obligation to produce logs to CERT-In is adhered to ... in a reasonable time") — [CERT-In Directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf); [CERT-In FAQs](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/FAQs_on_CyberSecurityDirections_May2022.pdf)
- SPDI Rule 5(4): no retention longer than required for the purpose — [SPDI Rules PDF](https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2011/IT_Rules_2011.pdf)

### Inferences
- Tension to flag for the report: the app's "account deletion" erases the user's data immediately; Rule 8(3) (after ~May 2027) arguably requires keeping the personal data, traffic data and processing logs for one year from processing even after deletion/withdrawal, and Rule 6(1)(e) also references one year of logs and "personal data". Read narrowly, a compliant design is: delete the user-facing data and content immediately, but retain access/processing logs (event metadata, not content) for 12 months in restricted storage, state this in the notice, then purge. Whether the full health-entry content must be held for a year is genuinely ambiguous (interpretation; no guidance).
- This directly conflicts with an EU GDPR erasure right; the EU researcher should be told the Indian floor is for logs/traffic data and is "unless... law" qualified.
- The Third Schedule inactivity-erasure and 48-hour notice do not apply; adopting a voluntary inactivity policy is optional good practice, not required.
- Log retention: CERT-In 180 days vs DPDP 1 year: one-year retention satisfies both. Supabase us-east-2 hosting means primary log copy is outside India; FAQ allows it if producible to CERT-In in reasonable time (interpretation for the small-operator case).

### Gaps
- No Board/MeitY guidance on whether Rule 8(3)'s "personal data" includes full content or only transactional/access data, nor how it squares with s.12(3)/s.8(7) erasure.
- Whether Rule 8(3) applies to a fiduciary whose users are not accessed by the State — the text has no size or sector limiter.

## 7. Children's data and the 18+ age gate

### Takeaway
A "child" is under 18 (s.2(f)); processing a child's data requires verifiable parental consent (s.9(1), Rule 10) and tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children are barred (s.9(3)). An 18+ only app is out of s.9 only if it actually keeps minors out; the Rules prescribe no specific age-verification method for adult-only services.

### Cited Findings
- s.2(f): child = individual who has not completed eighteen years — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- s.9: (1) verifiable parental/guardian consent before processing a child's personal data; (2) no processing likely to harm the child's well-being; (3) no tracking or behavioural monitoring of, or targeted advertising directed at, children; (4) prescribed class/purpose exemptions; (5) Central Government may notify an age above which a fiduciary is exempt — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf); [dpdpa.com s.9 summary](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section9.html)
- Rule 10(1): adopt "appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure that verifiable consent of the parent is obtained before the processing of any personal data of a child" and observe due diligence that the parent is an identifiable adult by reference to reliable identity/age details held by the fiduciary or voluntarily provided, including via a virtual token from an authorised entity (e.g., DigiLocker) — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Fourth Schedule Part A exempts only clinical/mental health establishments, healthcare professionals, allied healthcare professionals, educational institutions and similar named classes from s.9(1),(3) for restricted purposes; no consumer-app class — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Penalty for breach of s.9: up to Rs 200 crore (Schedule item 3) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Rules contain no obligation to age-verify adults on an 18+ service; Rule 10 applies when a fiduciary is processing a child's data — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf) (absence in text)
- Removal-of-Difficulties Order 2026 fixed s.9(1) wording to "personal data of a child or of a person with a disability" — [CyberPeace](https://cyberpeace.org/resources/blogs/small-words-big-consequences-reading-the-dpdp-removal-of-difficulties-order-2026)

### Inferences
- An 18+ declaration (date of birth gate, terms-of-use statement) is a reasonable good-faith measure but a self-declaration; because the data is also tobacco-related and tracking-based, a known minor user (e.g., user discloses age, support ticket) triggers s.9: stop processing, delete, or obtain verifiable parental consent. Build a documented under-18 handling path (block/delete on discovery). Whether self-declared DOB defeats a later Board finding of processing children's data is untested (interpretation).
- Notable because s.9(3) bars behavioural monitoring of children and the app's core is behaviour tracking: operating without an effective age gate is high-risk (penalty tier Rs 200 crore).
- A person with a lawful guardian and disability also needs verifiable consent (Rule 11) — edge case.

### Gaps
- No official guidance on acceptable age-assurance for adult-only apps.
- Rule 11/12 details not read in full.

## 8. Cross-border transfer, processors, security safeguards

### Takeaway
India uses a negative-list model: s.16 lets the Government restrict transfers to notified countries (none notified as of mid-2026 per secondary sources), s.16(2) preserves stricter sectoral laws, and Rule 15 only conditions transfers on any Government order about making data available to foreign States/agencies. The US storage with Supabase and sending data to Anthropic is permitted; what matters is the processor contract (s.8(2)), Rule 6 security safeguards, and the SPDI Rule 7 conditions that apply until ~May 2027.

### Cited Findings
- s.16(1): Central Government may by notification restrict transfer "to such country or territory outside India as may be so notified"; s.16(2): stricter existing laws prevail — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Rule 15: personal data processed under the Act "may be transferred outside the territory of India subject to the restriction that the Data Fiduciary shall meet such requirements as the Central Government may, by general or special order, specify in respect of making such personal data available to any foreign State, or to any person or entity under the control of or any agency of such a State" — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- No country notified under s.16 as of mid-2026 (secondary): "no country or territory has been notified as restricted" — [Mondaq (Samagra/other firm)](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1764976/from-localisation-debates-to-a-negative-list-making-cross-border-data-transfers-work-under-indias-dpdp-act); [Button Down/Desk summary](https://buttondown.com/the_desk/archive/no-country-is-restricted-your-data-still-cannot/); status after Aug 2026 not verified. Reported MeitY proposal (Jan 2026) to enforce Rule 13(4)/15 restrictions early and for SDFs — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/)
- Rule 13(4) data-localisation duty is limited to Significant Data Fiduciaries — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Processors: s.8(2) a fiduciary may engage a Data Processor "only under a valid contract"; s.8(1) fiduciary remains responsible for processors' processing; Rule 6(1)(f) requires "appropriate provision in the contract ... for taking reasonable security safeguards" — [dpdpa.com s.8 summary](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section8.html); [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 6(1) minimum security safeguards: (a) encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens; (b) access controls on computer resources used by fiduciary/processor; (c) logs, monitoring and review to detect unauthorised access; (d) continuity measures such as data backups; (e) retain logs and personal data one year; (f) processor contract security provisions; (g) technical and organisational measures to ensure effective observance. Penalty for breach of s.8(5): up to Rs 250 crore (Schedule item 1) — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf); [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Until commencement, SPDI Rule 7 permits transfer of SPDI abroad only to a recipient "that ensures the same level of data protection" and only if necessary for performing the contract or with the person's consent — [SPDI Rules PDF](https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2011/IT_Rules_2011.pdf)
- Sectoral stricter laws (e.g., RBI payment data) do not apply to a cigarette tracker; no health-sector localisation law was found for consumer wellness apps (absence of evidence) — see [Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas blog](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2024/06/mind-your-meds-and-metrics-navigating-the-indian-health-data-protection-labyrinth/) for health-data regime discussion (2024, pre-Rules).

### Inferences
- Disclosures to include in the notice/processor list: Supabase (US, us-east-2) for storage; Anthropic (US) for opt-in AI chat. Rely on consent for the AI transfer (explicit per-purpose) to meet SPDI Rule 7's consent route now, and on a contract with security terms (DPAs with Supabase and Anthropic) to meet s.8(2)/Rule 6(1)(f).
- Only a hypothetical future s.16 notification or Rule 15 order naming the US would change the legal position; monitor MeitY.
- Rule 6(1)(a) encryption/masking: supports encrypting notes at rest and not sending identifiers to Anthropic.

### Gaps
- Official MeitY confirmation that no s.16 notification exists as of Nov 2026 not retrieved.
- No Rule or guidance specifies minimum DPA content with processors beyond security safeguards.

## 9. Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF)

### Takeaway
Smoke Ledger is almost certainly not an SDF: SDF status is by Central Government notification (s.10(1)) on volume/sensitivity/risk factors, there is no numeric threshold in the Act or Rules, and no notification naming a class that would capture a small health-habit app was found.

### Cited Findings
- s.10(1): Central Government "may notify any Data Fiduciary or class of Data Fiduciaries as Significant Data Fiduciary" based on factors incl. (a) volume and sensitivity of data, (b) risk to rights of Data Principal, (c) sovereignty, (d) electoral democracy, (e) security of the State, (f) public order. s.10(2): DPO based in India, independent data auditor, periodic DPIA and audit — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Rule 13: annual DPIA and audit, report to the Board, algorithmic due diligence, possible data localisation for specified data — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf). Penalty for s.10 breach up to Rs 150 crore — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- The only numeric thresholds in the Rules are the Third Schedule retention classes (2 crore / 50 lakh / 2 crore registered users), which are not SDF thresholds — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf); sectors speculated as likely SDFs (healthcare, finance, large platforms) — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/) (speculation). Seventh Schedule item 3 shows MeitY will call for information for "assessment for notifying any Data Fiduciary ... as Significant Data Fiduciary" — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Because the DPO is mandatory only for SDFs ("where applicable"), a non-SDF need only publish a contact person (Rule 9) — [Rules Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)

### Inferences
- Not an SDF today. Risk is low but "sensitivity of data" is an express factor, so growth plus health-adjacent data could in theory attract designation; note the possibility only.

### Gaps
- No published SDF notification or criteria found; Nov 2026 status unverified.

## 10. Penalties and phased timeline

### Takeaway
Penalties are imposed by the Board after inquiry (s.33), capped per Schedule; the top tiers are Rs 250 crore (security safeguards) and Rs 200 crore (breach notification, children). All substantive tiers become enforceable only with the 18-month tranche. There is no criminal liability in the DPDP Act and no compensation right for Data Principals in the Act.

### Cited Findings
- Schedule (s.33(1)) caps: (1) failure of reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5)) up to Rs 250 crore; (2) failure to notify Board/Principals of breach (s.8(6)) up to Rs 200 crore; (3) children's obligations (s.9) up to Rs 200 crore; (4) SDF obligations (s.10) up to Rs 150 crore; (5) breach of Data Principal duties (s.15) up to Rs 10,000; (6) breach of voluntary undertaking (s.32); (7) any other provision of the Act or Rules up to Rs 50 crore — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- s.33(2) factors: nature, gravity, duration; type/nature of data; repetition; gain or loss avoided; mitigation and timeliness; proportionality; likely impact on the person (relevant to individual operators) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf)
- Board may accept a voluntary undertaking (s.32); appeals go to the TDSAT within 60 days (s.29; Rule 22 in the 18-month group) — [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf); [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)
- Timeline as of 2 Nov 2026: Phase I (13 Nov 2025) institutional provisions incl. Board, Rules 17-21; Phase II (~13 Nov 2026) Consent Manager registration (Rule 4, s.6(9)); Phase III (~13 May 2027) everything else incl. ss.3-17 (except 6(9)), Rules 3, 5-16, 22, 23, Board's penalty powers (s.27 save (1)(d), 28-34) and s.44(2) — [Acuity Law](https://acuitylaw.co.in/?p=6681); [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)
- IT Act s.43A compensation (negligent security of SPDI by body corporate) remains until s.44(2) commences — text of s.44(2) in [Act PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/what-happens-spdi-rules-after-dpdp-enforcement/)
- CERT-In non-compliance: "punitive action under sub-section (7) of the section 70B of the IT Act" — [CERT-In Directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- Individual-operator context: penalties are "may extend to", proportionate per s.33(2)(g); the practical exposure for a tiny app is reputational, store/platform and complaint-driven rather than crore-scale, but the legal cap is not reduced for size (no small-business tier in the Schedule).
- (s.43A compensation text itself was not retrieved; described from commentary and the s.44(2) text which omits it.)

### Gaps
- s.43A compensation ceiling wording not re-read from primary text.
- Whether the 18-month window was shortened by a notification after Feb 2026 cannot be excluded (see section 0).

## 11. COTPA 2003, app-store listing and CERT-In applicability

### Takeaway
COTPA s.5 bans tobacco advertising/promotion and appears not to reach a neutral tracking/quit-awareness app, but branded content (cigarette brand names, logos, pack imagery, "lifestyle" tone) is the exposure point; CERT-In's 2022 Directions (6-hour reporting, 180-day logs, NTP sync, point of contact) apply to "body corporate" including sole proprietors, though the FAQ excludes "individual citizens".

### Cited Findings
- COTPA s.5(1): "No person engaged in, or purported to be engaged in the production, supply or distribution of cigarettes or any other tobacco products shall advertise and no person having control over a medium shall cause to be advertised cigarettes or any other tobacco products through that medium and no person shall take part in any advertisement which directly or indirectly suggests or promotes the use or consumption of cigarettes or any other tobacco products." s.3(a) "advertisement" includes "any visible representation by way of notice, circular, label, wrapper or other document" and announcements by any means of producing or transmitting light, sound, smoke or gas. s.5(2): no person for direct or indirect pecuniary benefit shall display or permit display of tobacco advertisements. Penalty s.22: up to 2 years and/or Rs 1,000 fine for first conviction; up to 5 years and Rs 5,000 for subsequent — [COTPA PDF](https://www.goatourism.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/COTPA-2003.pdf) (OCR-noisy copy; quoted wording reconstructed from it)
- Commentary: whether "advertisement" reaches internet/app content is contested; a 2020 amendment bill would have expressly included electronic media/internet but I could not confirm enactment — [Ylcube analysis](https://ylcube.com/c/blogs/analysis-cigarettes-and-other-tobacco-products-amendment-bill-2020/); [Nyaaya explainer](https://nyaaya.org/legal-explainer/advertisement-of-tobacco-products/); [Tobacco Control Laws](https://tobaccocontrollaws.org/legislation/country/india/aps-regulated-forms)
- I found no COTPA provision, rule or reported enforcement action specific to a tobacco-use tracking or quit-support app, nor app-store rules specific to India (absence of evidence)
- CERT-In scope: Directions (i)-(iv) apply to "All service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporate and Government organisations"; FAQ Q7 lists these and states "Individual citizens are not covered by these Directions"; FAQ Q25 reproduces the s.43A definition of body corporate (includes sole proprietorship engaged in commercial/professional activity); FAQ Q26-Q29 say incident reporting applies to any entity, and foreign-based service providers offering services in India must designate a point of contact; FAQ Q36: any service provider offering services to users in India "needs to enable and maintain logs and records of financial transactions in Indian jurisdiction" — [CERT-In Directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf); [CERT-In FAQs](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/FAQs_on_CyberSecurityDirections_May2022.pdf)
- CERT-In duties: (i) sync ICT clocks to NIC/NPL NTP; (ii) report Annexure I incidents within 6 hours; (iii) designate a Point of Contact (Annexure II format to info@cert-in.org.in); (iv) "mandatorily enable logs of all their ICT systems and maintain them securely for a rolling period of 180 days ... within the Indian jurisdiction" — FAQ Q35 relaxes: may be stored outside India if producible to CERT-In in reasonable time — [CERT-In Directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf); [CERT-In FAQs](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/FAQs_on_CyberSecurityDirections_May2022.pdf). Effective after 60 days from 28 Apr 2022. The FAQ extended timelines for MSMEs etc. to 25 Sept 2022 per a secondary source — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/it-and-internet/1202576/everything-you-need-to-know-about-indias-new-guidelines-related-to-cyber-incident-reporting-by-cert-in) (low-confidence, historical)
- Cloud/VPS provider KYC duty (Direction v) is on those providers (Supabase/Vercel-type), not on the app operator — [CERT-In Directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- COTPA hygiene for listings and UI: avoid cigarette brand logos, pack images and promotional language that "suggests or promotes" consumption; use generic categories or text-only brand names if brand logging is needed; no sponsored or affiliate tobacco content; a quit-support/harm-reduction tone in store listing and screenshots. (Interpretation; COTPA's "indirectly promotes" language is broad and untested for apps.)
- CERT-In: an individual operator running a commercial app is plausibly a "body corporate" (s.43A definition) or "service provider"/"intermediary" under the Directions; treat the 6-hour reporting, point-of-contact registration and NTP/180-day logging as applicable (conservative reading). The FAQ's "individual citizens" carve-out is for non-commercial individuals (interpretation).
- Supabase hosted in us-east-2: logs sit outside India; compliance relies on FAQ Q35 (ability to produce on request) — document that the operator can export logs promptly.

- Not found: any official CERT-In statement on small commercial sole-proprietor app operators; any COTPA case on apps; whether Google Play/App Store impose India-specific tobacco listing rules.
- COTPA amendment bill 2020 enactment status unconfirmed.

## 12. Consolidated checklist (what to do and disclose) — status tags: NOW = legally in force Nov 2026; MAY-2027 = DPDP Rules/Act tranche; BOTH = good practice that satisfies both

1. Standalone, plain-language notice at each consent point, itemised data and specific purposes and features; app/website link; withdrawal, rights, Board-complaint routes — Act s.5, Rule 3 [MAY-2027]; SPDI Rule 4/5(3) privacy policy and collection-time disclosure [NOW]. Offer English + Eighth Schedule languages — s.5(3) [MAY-2027].
2. Separate opt-in consent for analytics and AI chat; unticked, no service denial, limited to necessary data; log consent version/timestamp for burden of proof — s.6(1),(10) [MAY-2027]; SPDI Rule 5(1),(7) written (email/in-app) consent for SPDI [NOW].
3. In-app withdrawal as easy as giving; on withdrawal stop processing, tell processors (Anthropic) — s.6(4),(6) [MAY-2027]; SPDI Rule 5(7) [NOW].
4. Publish: rights-request means and identifiers (Rule 14(1)); grievance process with response period not exceeding 90 days (Rule 14(3)); contact person (Rule 9); nominee mechanism (Rule 14(4)) [MAY-2027]. Name a Grievance Officer with one-month limit (SPDI Rule 5(9)) [NOW].
5. Reasonable security safeguards, minimum list of Rule 6(1)(a)-(g), documented security programme (SPDI Rule 8) — Rule 6 [MAY-2027]; SPDI Rule 8 [NOW].
6. Processor contracts (Supabase, Anthropic) with security provisions and erasure-on-instruction — s.8(2), Rule 6(1)(f) [MAY-2027].
7. Breach runbook: CERT-In 6h [NOW]; Board "without delay" plus 72h detailed report; users notified with five items — Rule 7 [MAY-2027].
8. Retain logs 1 year (Rules 6(1)(e), 8(3)) and erase after, unless other law; CERT-In 180-day logs; NTP sync; point of contact [CERT-In NOW; Rules MAY-2027].
9. 18+ gate and under-18 handling path (s.9, Rule 10) [MAY-2027].
10. Cross-border: disclose US storage/processing; track s.16 notifications and Rule 15 orders; SPDI Rule 7 consent/necessity route [NOW]; no localisation duty (not an SDF).
11. Legacy-user notice after commencement for already-consenting users — s.5(2) [MAY-2027]; do not rely on s.7(a) for optional features.
12. COTPA hygiene in app content and store listing; no brand promotion [NOW].
13. Monitor: MeitY gazette for any compression of the 18-month window, SDF notifications, s.17(3) startup exemptions, s.16 negative list, Board complaint portal.
